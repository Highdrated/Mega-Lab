## Ladder rule (her words, 24 Nov 2026)
"the next lesson is a gradual step from the previous so i should not learn about entra then jump to shell scripts the next ladder step - the user or me should already have a general idea of which direction to go in otherwise the helper nearby"
- The School follows LADDER in data.js, not the exam's order: 1 tenant, 2 identities, 3 sign-in, 4 Graph PowerShell, 5 workloads, 6 Defender, 7 Purview, 8 Copilot, 9 agents, 10 agent guard, 11 monitoring.
- Every step has a "builds on" line naming the earlier steps it rests on, and Lumen's new-words box (gloss) for terms it introduces.
- A new tool (like PowerShell) only comes after the same job was done by hand.
- Cases say which School steps they build on, and only unlock after those steps.
- Inside a step, lessons and checks also go from familiar to new, one idea at a time.

## Good habits (her words, 24 Nov 2026)
"while building and adding content remember that the lab needs to teach good habit, by that i mean if it does not work in real life it should not work in this lab, tab to auto complete and other similar habits that should be used on a daily basis as a microsoft engineer"
- Every command box behaves like the real console: Tab completes cmdlets, parameters, enum values (-LockState) and $true/$false; Tab again cycles, Shift+Tab goes back; no match changes nothing. The PowerShell window wraps lines at the edge like a real console.
- Habit stamps in the PowerShell window (Tab, History, Checked) and on the case-closed screen. Lumen nudges once when a long parameter is typed out in full. New cases must reward the same habits (look before you change, check after you change, least privilege, report-only or simulation first).
